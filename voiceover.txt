Wi-Fi is light, so why does it go through walls?
It's the same stuff your eyes see, just stretched.
Its waves are about a hundred thousand times longer, and the longer the wave, the weaker each little packet of light.
Now, a wall is full of electrons, and they only take light that can kick them up a step.
The light you see can, so the wall eats it.
Wi-Fi is far too weak to kick anything, so the electrons let it slip through.
In glass, the steps are too high even for the light you see, which is why you can see through it.
So to your Wi-Fi, a brick wall is a window.
Metal is the opposite.
Its electrons are loose, so they grab both kinds of light and throw them right back.
That's why metal is shiny and why your signal dies in an elevator.
